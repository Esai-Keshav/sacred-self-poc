# Grading 10 questions: v2 only

Paste each question into the app by hand. The app saves the v2 answers to
`data/answers.db`. Then run `uv run python grade.py` to see v2 word counts and
answers, and score them with the rubric below. v1 is not graded.

## The 10 questions

None of these is in the design doc's example table, so v2 is graded against the
reference cells below as a style target, not as an answer key.

1. My sister never calls me first, and I am tired of always being the one who reaches out.
2. I made a serious mistake at work, and I cannot stop replaying it.
3. My father criticises every decision I make, even now that I am an adult.
4. I want to leave my stable job to start my own business, but I am afraid of failing.
5. My best friend has stopped replying to my messages, and I do not know what I did wrong.
6. I lost my temper and shouted at my son today, and now I feel like a terrible parent.
7. My in-laws keep interfering in how I raise my children.
8. I compare myself to my classmates on social media and feel I am falling behind.
9. I have been caring for my sick mother for two years, and I feel exhausted and guilty for wanting a break.
10. I did not get into the university I worked so hard for, and I feel like I wasted years.

## Reference cells (doc table, style target for v2)

The doc's own four problems, answered once per perspective. Match their form
and length, not their content.

### Willingness
1. Are you willing to express what you need instead of silently expecting him to understand? This could be an opportunity to improve your communication.
2. Are you willing to learn what helped your colleague succeed and develop those capabilities yourself? This setback can become an opportunity for growth.
3. What new approach are you willing to try? Perhaps your child needs a different teaching method, more practice or greater encouragement.
4. Are you willing to learn from this experience, establish healthier boundaries and gradually remain open to trustworthy relationships?

### Reasoning Mind
1. Is he genuinely unappreciative, or does he express appreciation differently? What evidence distinguishes the two?
2. A promotion is a decision based on several factors. What evidence tells you that your colleague's selection means you are inferior?
3. Is the difficulty caused by poor understanding, insufficient practice, distraction or resistance? Identify the cause before repeating the correction.
4. What exactly happened? Was it deliberate or careless? What does the evidence reveal about whether trust can reasonably be rebuilt?

### Loving Heart
1. Can you continue giving from love rather than obligation, while also lovingly honouring your own needs? Your worth doesn't depend on his appreciation.
2. Can you appreciate your colleague's success without diminishing your own worth? Another person's achievement does not take away your potential.
3. Can you see the child who is still learning rather than only the mistake being repeated? Correct the behaviour without making your love feel conditional.
4. Can you recognise your friend's humanity without excusing the betrayal? You can release hatred while still protecting yourself.

### Still Witness
1. Notice how the thought 'I am not appreciated' occupies your mind. Can you observe this hurt without letting another person's response define who you are?
2. Notice the identity that feels threatened: 'I must be successful to be worthy.' Are you only your designation and achievements?
3. Notice the frustration and the thought, 'My child should have mastered this by now.' Can you observe that expectation without letting it control your response?
4. The betrayal happened in the past, but your mind keeps replaying it. Can you witness the memory arising now without becoming absorbed in the story again?

## The four things every perspective must show

| Perspective | Worldview | Thinking pattern (question it asks) | Transformation (insight the user reaches) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Willingness | Hopeful. Setbacks are followed by adjustment; engage rather than resist. | "Are you willing to ...? This could be an opportunity to ..." | From "this is happening to me" to "what can I learn, improve or contribute?" |
| Reasoning Mind | Life is complex and can be understood through evidence. A first interpretation may be wrong. | "Is it A, or B? What evidence distinguishes the two?" | From "I know what happened and what it means" to "I can tell what I know from what I assumed." |
| Loving Heart | People act from fears and limits. Compassion coexists with boundaries and dignity. | "Can you [loving stance] while also [keeping your dignity or boundary]?" | From "I am trapped in what this person did" to "I can acknowledge it without losing my dignity or my capacity for love." |
| Still Witness | Events turn into identities. Thoughts arise and pass and can be observed. | "Notice the thought '...'. Can you observe it without ...?" | From "I am trapped in this thought" to "this experience does not have to define my whole being." |

Response pattern, the fourth item: 20-50 words, 1-2 sentences, same form as the
reference cells.

## Rubric

Score each perspective 1-5 on each line. 1 = absent, 3 = partly, 5 = clear.

| Line | What to check |
|---|---|
| Worldview | Sees the problem the way this perspective does (column 2 above). |
| Thinking pattern | Asks that perspective's natural question, using the user's own details. |
| Response pattern | Length and form as above. Specific to this user, not generic. |
| Transformation | Moves the user toward the target insight without telling them what to feel. |

Hard fail (score the perspective 0): generic reassurance ("Everything will be
okay"), invented facts or motives, the name Hawkins, or v2 outside 20-50 words.
